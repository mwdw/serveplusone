The Sinner & Alcaraz Tax

Sinner and Alcaraz tax the top 20 the way they tax everyone else. And nobody can tax them back.

Much has been made of how much further ahead of the pack Alcaraz and Sinner are in men's tennis. The simplest tell is the rankings:

2-rankings.jpg

But it's instructive to look at how that gap has opened up in terms of performance.

Here's what happens when the current top 20 ranked players (excluding Sinner and Alcaraz) play each other when hitting a 1st serve, next to what happens when those same players meet Sinner or Alcaraz:

1-first-serve-tax.jpg

- 6.1% decrease in aces or unreturned serves
- 2% fewer forehand +1 opportunities
- 6.5% decrease in points won when hitting a forehand after their serve
- 8.4%(!) decrease in points won when hitting a backhand after their serve
- and overall a 7.5% decrease in 1st serve points won

In a sport where percentage points of performance can make or break wins and losses, these are huge dents being made. And these dents aren't being made against just anybody, they're against the next 18 best tennis players on earth, the elite.

Here's what happens on 2nd serve:

3-second-serve-tax.jpg

- On average, any 2nd serve that comes back becomes a losing play
- 55% of the time when a top 20 player hits a forehand after a 2nd serve vs Alcaraz or Sinner they lose the point
- 60% of the time when a top 20 player hits a backhand after a 2nd serve vs Alcaraz or Sinner they lose the point
- Backhand +1s become more common than forehands against Sinner and Alcaraz when hitting 2nd serves

Alcaraz and Sinner really are just much better than the field, on 1st serve return especially:

4-first-serve-return.jpg

Perhaps most extraordinarily of all, Sinner and Alcaraz have pushed so far ahead of the chasing pack that neither of them, when playing even half-decent, seem to notice much of a difference between a top 100 player and a top 20 player. Sinner and Alcaraz actually perform slightly better against top 20 players on 1st serve than against players ranked 21–100:

5-top-20-vs-21-100.jpg

Sinner and Alcaraz tax the top 20 the way they tax everyone else. And nobody can tax them back. From the altitude those two players have reached, everything below them (apart from Djokovic on a good day) reads flat. Rank 8 and rank 41 may as well sit at the same elevation.

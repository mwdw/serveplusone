Where a 1st serve does the most damage

The thing missing from [this](/posts/how-close-to-the-line) was 1st serve depth, which matters on wide serves and T serves for different reasons. Looking at 7 years of Australian Open serve data you see two intuitive things. 1. the impact of different serve speeds and accuracy to the side/T lines on unreturned serves. And 2. out wide, the shorter in the box the better, down the T, the deeper the better. This depth isn't accounted for by the '1st serve accuracy' stat that's gotten attention this year

1-damage-map.jpg

Players can get to similar serve outcomes in different ways. Better speed, accuracy and depth all make a serve harder to return, but in different amounts especially as you get closer to the lines. Out wide, depth probably matters more than most people think: landing shorter in the box does more for unreturned rates than hitting faster within normal ranges, at the same distance from the sideline

2-two-routes.jpg

Over the last 52 weeks Fritz lands his deuce-side wide serve the shortest in the box of the top 10, and hits it slower to get there (112mph to this wide spot vs his 123mph 1st-serve average). Sinner hits his wide spot 6mph faster than Fritz (118mph), deeper, and slightly closer to the line, though both are v accurate

Both of these serves are incredibly effective, and both go unreturned about 50% of the time, but Fritz’s is safer: 77% land in vs Sinner’s 68%

Their avg spot markers may look close together (2nd image), but Fritz’s shorter landing and extra deviation contribute to both serves being similarly damaging despite the slower, and in this case safer, serve

Summary: 1st serve depth matters on wide serves and T serves for different reasons: out wide, the shorter in the box the better; down the T, the deeper the better.
